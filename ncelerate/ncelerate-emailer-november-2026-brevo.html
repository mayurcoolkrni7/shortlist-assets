If you’re weighing an India GCC, one of the first calls you’ll make is who builds it and who owns it. This month, three well-known U.S. companies made that call three different ways. Here’s what happened, and how NCelerate can help.

Three things worth knowing
Mars: start with a partner, take ownership later
Mars opened its Hyderabad GCC with Genpact, its operations partner since 2018 (reported Nov. 23). The roughly 60,000-square-foot center opens with 400+ Genpact staff and plans to transition to 1,000 Mars associates. It will redesign Mars operations around AI-assisted workflows.
Why it matters: you get running quickly without tying yourself to a vendor for good. Before you sign, agree on when the handover happens and what triggers it.
Blackstone: its own people, plus partners
Blackstone opened a GCC in Bengaluru, its second India office (announced Oct. 28). The center brings together Blackstone employees and professional services partners, and supports finance, technology, HR, legal and compliance across the firm’s global businesses.
Why it matters: a mixed team lets you keep sensitive work close while partners handle the rest. It works when the line between the two is drawn clearly from day one.
Gilead: bring the work in-house
Gilead Sciences opened Gilead Capabilities Center India in Bengaluru earlier this month. The goal is to bring more technology and digital expertise in-house, starting with cybersecurity and AI.
Why it matters: when a biopharma company starts its India center with cybersecurity and AI, it shows how far GCCs have moved past back-office work. Full ownership gives you control, but you carry setup and hiring yourself.
